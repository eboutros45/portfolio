## Overview

IDLab is built around one idea: the repetitive parts of creative and media work should be handled by software, so attention can stay on the work itself.

Its core functionality works today. Some experimental features are still being explored and are not considered complete.

## The problem

Music and digital media work involves many small, repetitive steps spread across different tools. Done by hand, these tasks are slow and error-prone, and they take attention away from the work that actually matters.

IDLab explores how much of that repetition can be reliably handed over to software.

## My role

- Identifying repetitive tasks worth automating
- Designing and developing the application
- Building integrations between software tools
- Exploring and testing experimental features

## Product progression

IDLab established the original foundation for [Backroom](/projects/backroom). Backroom continues the product journey by evolving the underlying music-management and DJ workflows into a more accessible, web-based experience.

The two remain distinct projects: IDLab is the original application, while Backroom represents the next stage of the idea.

## What I learned

Automation is only valuable when it is dependable. Edge cases decide whether a tool actually saves time.

Integrating with external software means designing around constraints you do not control.

Keeping stable features separate from experiments keeps a tool usable while it continues to evolve.
